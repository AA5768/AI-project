# EFEM Cost Reduction Workshop

Yuki Tanaka: Gross margin on Helios-3 at the current bill of materials is 41 percent against a company target of 52. This workshop exists to find eight to ten points. Owen has the cost breakdown.

Owen Brady: Standard cost per EFEM is 366,000 dollars against a list price of 620,000. The top five lines are the two harmonic drive reducers at 8,400 including the expedite premium, the linear encoder pair at 21,000, the ceramic blades at 3,100 for a set, the fan filter units at 34,000 for the set of six, and the frame weldment and precision machining at 58,000.

Greg Salinas: Plus about 62,000 of labour and cleanroom overhead, which is the line nobody puts on a slide.

Yuki Tanaka: Start with the biggest.

Tomas Lindqvist: The frame is the biggest single line and it is the easiest to attack, because it is over-designed. It is a welded steel frame with machined datums on six faces and we specified it when we did not yet know which faces mattered. Three of them do not.

Owen Brady: Saving?

Tomas Lindqvist: Dropping three machined datums is about 11,000 dollars a frame. It requires requalifying the alignment procedure, which is two weeks of my time and a repeatability re-verification.

Ingrid Lund: Requalifying alignment does not touch particles, so I have no objection.

Yuki Tanaka: Take it. That is 11,000 of the roughly 60,000 we need.

Greg Salinas: Fan filter units. Thirty-four thousand for six is the specification Ingrid wrote, ISO Class 2 in the box.

Ingrid Lund: I will defend that number. The reason we win on particles is the downflow, and the reason Northgate did not cancel us in April is that we could show the box recovers to baseline in under ninety seconds. Cheaper fan filter units means a slower recovery and it is the thing customers measure at acceptance.

Yuki Tanaka: I am not asking you to give up performance. I am asking whether six units at that grade is the only way to get that performance.

Ingrid Lund: That is a different question and it is a fair one. Five units with a redesigned plenum might do it. I would need to model it, and I would want to measure it on a real box before I signed anything.

Tomas Lindqvist: The plenum redesign is not free. Call it three weeks of design and a new sheet metal tool at 18,000.

Owen Brady: Saving five to six thousand a machine, so the tool pays back in three or four machines.

Yuki Tanaka: Fund it, with Ingrid's measurement as the gate. If the box does not recover in ninety seconds we keep six units and we lose the 18,000. I will take that risk.

Ingrid Lund: On that basis I will run the model. I want it stated in the decision that I hold the veto on the measurement.

Yuki Tanaka: It is stated.

Nadia Haddad: Encoders at 21,000 a pair are the second biggest line and they are going away anyway, because Brunnen is end-of-lifing the head. Rev B has to pick a replacement. If we pick on cost as well as on performance we can be at 13,000 to 15,000.

Tomas Lindqvist: That is real, and it is the strongest argument for starting Rev B that I have heard, better than the throughput argument.

Yuki Tanaka: Priya asked me in June to restore Rev B funding in FY26 and I said I would decide in July. This is July. Nadia, if Rev B takes 6,000 dollars out of every machine at the encoder line alone, what is the volume next year?

Nadia Haddad: FY27 plan is 84 EFEMs.

Yuki Tanaka: Five hundred thousand dollars a year at the encoder line. Then Rev B is funded, 200,000 dollars, in FY26 starting in August. Owen, move it.

Owen Brady: I will move 200,000 back into the Helios-3 NRE line and reforecast. The line goes from 3.2 million to 3.4 million, not back to 3.6, because the second tooling set stays in FY27.

Greg Salinas: Last one from me, and it is labour. Sixty-two thousand of labour and overhead per machine is high because we build in a Class 5 bay that costs what it costs and we do work in there that does not need to be in there. Cable harness assembly does not need Class 5. Frame assembly does not need Class 5.

Ingrid Lund: Agreed on harnesses if they are bagged before they enter. Frame assembly I would want to see the process before I agreed.

Greg Salinas: Moving harness and frame out of the clean bay saves about 9,000 a machine and it also frees clean bay hours, which is the ceiling we keep hitting.

Yuki Tanaka: Do the harnesses now and bring me the frame question with Ingrid's process review in September.

Owen Brady: Adding it up: 11,000 frame datums, 5,500 fan filter units subject to the measurement, 6,000 encoder in Rev B, 9,000 harness relocation. That is 31,500, which takes standard cost to about 334,000 and margin from 41 to 46 percent.

Yuki Tanaka: Forty-six is not 52. But it is the honest number and I would rather report 46 with four funded actions than 52 with a wish. Owen, build the tracker and we review monthly.

Owen Brady: I will have a cost reduction tracker with owners and dates by the end of next week.
