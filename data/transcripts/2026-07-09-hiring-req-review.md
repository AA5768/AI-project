# Engineering Hiring Requisition Review

Sofia Marchetti: Five open requisitions, one of which is new since the budget review. I will go through them and I need decisions on two.

Sofia Marchetti: Senior motion control engineer. Reopened at the new band of 195,000 on the twenty-second of May. Nineteen applicants, four screened, two in final round. One candidate has a competing offer and needs an answer by Friday.

Priya Raman: Which one?

Sofia Marchetti: The candidate with the servo drive background from a wafer inspection company. Dana and Tomas both scored her strongly. The panel concern was that she has never worked on a machine that goes in a cleanroom.

Priya Raman: Dana had never worked in a cleanroom either when we hired him. Make the offer at the top of the band and make it today.

Sofia Marchetti: At 195 exactly, or is there room?

Priya Raman: At 195 with a signing bonus if you need it to close. Not above the band, because we just levelled two people to fix compression and I am not breaking that in the same quarter.

Sofia Marchetti: I will get the offer out this afternoon.

Sofia Marchetti: Second contamination control engineer. Open since February, band is fine, and the problem is supply. There are maybe forty people in the country with real fab contamination control experience who are not already at a large equipment company.

Priya Raman: Ingrid is a single point of failure on the one thing that killed us at Northgate. I do not want to still be saying that in January.

Sofia Marchetti: Then consider the alternative I raised in March, which you did not like. Hire a strong mechanical engineer with cleanroom exposure and have Ingrid train them, and use a consultant for the first six months for the metrology-heavy work.

Priya Raman: I did not like it because a consultant is not on the hook at three in the morning when a customer's tool is down. But I have not produced a better idea in four months. Do it. Hire for aptitude, bring in the consultant for six months, and Ingrid builds the second person.

Sofia Marchetti: I will rewrite the requisition this week and get a consultant statement of work from two firms.

Owen Brady: The consultant is not in the FY26 plan. What am I booking?

Sofia Marchetti: Six months at roughly 1,400 dollars a day, two days a week. About 73,000 dollars.

Owen Brady: Then it is a budget amendment and Yuki has to see it.

Priya Raman: Put it in front of her with the Northgate excursion cost next to it. Two hundred and ten thousand dollars for one excursion is the argument.

Sofia Marchetti: Third, field service engineer for APAC, based in Penang. This one is close. Offer out, verbal acceptance, start date the first of September.

Aisha Bello: That one matters more than people think. Right now every Kestrel service call in Asia is Victor getting on a plane, and Victor is an applications engineer, not a service engineer. It is expensive and it is the wrong person.

Sofia Marchetti: Fourth and fifth are the two Vantage software engineers. Both filled, both starting in August. Hassan is running the onboarding.

Priya Raman: Good. Sofia, the question I keep not asking. What is our attrition looking like?

Sofia Marchetti: Voluntary attrition is 11 percent annualised, which is normal for us. Two departures this year that concerned me were both in operations, both citing the shift pattern in the cleanroom rather than pay.

Greg Salinas: I know about that. When we go from four builds a month to seven, the shift pattern gets worse before it gets better, and the new cleanroom bay is eleven weeks out.

Sofia Marchetti: Then I would rather plan a hiring wave into operations now than backfill two more resignations in October.

Greg Salinas: Give me two cleanroom technicians in the requisition list and I will make the shift pattern work.

Priya Raman: Add them. Sofia, bring the two technician requisitions to the next review with a cost so Owen can put it in the forecast.
