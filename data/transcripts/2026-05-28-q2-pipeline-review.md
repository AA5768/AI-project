# Q2 Sales Pipeline Review

Ryan Chen: Pipeline review. Total qualified pipeline is 21.4 million dollars against a second-half plan of 28.9. I will go deal by deal on the five that matter and then we talk about the gap.

Ryan Chen: Northgate Micro. Pilot tool installed, particle excursion in April, tool recovered and running since the fifteenth. The production order is eleven EFEMs, about 6.4 million dollars. Their equipment engineering director told me last week that the order is contingent on a clean 90-day run on the pilot and on the 8D being closed to their satisfaction.

Aisha Bello: The 8D went to them on the thirtieth of April and they have not formally accepted it. They asked two follow-up questions about the supplier corrective action and Nadia answered both on the eighth of May.

Ryan Chen: So it is sitting with them. I will push for formal closure this week, because I do not want it open in September when they are making the order decision.

Yuki Tanaka: What is your honest probability on Northgate?

Ryan Chen: Sixty percent. It was eighty-five before April. The excursion did not lose it, but it turned a formality into a decision.

Victor Nwosu: I would say sixty-five. The thing that recovered it was that Ingrid went on a plane the same week and found two causes instead of arguing for a month.

Ryan Chen: Cobalt Foundry Group. Second-source EFEM evaluation, install scheduled for the eleventh of August, potential of eighteen units over two years, call it 9.8 million. Their evaluation criteria are particles, footprint, and E187 cybersecurity conformance on the software side.

Marcus Feld: E187 is partially in Vantage 4.0. Not all of it.

Victor Nwosu: Their purchase specification cites the standard, and I sent Elena the exact clause in April. What they will actually audit is the edge agent's privilege model, which is in 4.0.

Ryan Chen: Tallgrass Semiconductor. Four Kestrel-2 handlers, 1.4 million, decision at the end of September. Blocked on contactor life. Victor has an eight-week data commitment.

Victor Nwosu: Data back to them by the middle of August. The work is funded and Tomas has the brackets ordered.

Ryan Chen: Verdant Power Semi. Vantage only, on-premises, roughly 340,000 a year. Blocked on on-premises deployment, which is 4.1. They have asked twice for a date and I have not given one.

Marcus Feld: Do not give one. That is my instruction and I will take the consequence if it costs us the deal this year.

Ryan Chen: Fifth is Halden Photonics, a new logo, small, two Kestrel units for a compound semiconductor line, about 680,000. Early stage, no blockers, no reason to think it is real yet.

Yuki Tanaka: So the second-half plan is 28.9 and the weighted pipeline is what?

Owen Brady: Weighted at Ryan's probabilities it is 11.2 million. Unweighted qualified is 21.4. Against a 28.9 plan, both numbers say the plan does not close on the current pipeline.

Yuki Tanaka: Then let us be specific about what the gap is, because "sell more" is not a plan. Is this a demand problem or a capacity problem?

Ryan Chen: Honestly, it is neither. It is a concentration problem. Two deals, Northgate and Cobalt, are 16.2 million of the 21.4. If both land we beat the plan. If one slips a quarter we miss it badly.

Victor Nwosu: And both of them are decided by the same two or three technical criteria, particles and conformance, which is at least something we control.

Marcus Feld: What would change the concentration? More logos at the small end, which is Halden, or a services attach we are not selling.

Aisha Bello: We are not selling services. Every preventive maintenance contract we have was negotiated as a concession during a hardware deal. Nobody has ever sold one on its own.

Owen Brady: Service revenue is 4 percent of total. For a company with 40 installed handlers in the field, the benchmark is closer to 15.

Yuki Tanaka: That is the most interesting number anyone has said today. Aisha, what would it take to sell a real service offering?

Aisha Bello: A defined offering with a price, a spares model, and one person whose job it is. Today it is me, in the gaps.

Yuki Tanaka: Marcus, work with Aisha on a service offering proposal for the FY27 plan. Not this quarter, but I want it in the planning input.

Marcus Feld: I will take it as an FY27 planning input and bring a draft offering in October.
