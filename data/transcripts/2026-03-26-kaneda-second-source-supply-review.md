# Kaneda Second Source Supply Review

Nadia Haddad: Three long-lead items and one of them got worse this week. I will go in order of how much they can hurt us.

Nadia Haddad: Kaneda Precision harmonic drive reducers. We use two per Helios-3 arm, theta and R. Quoted lead time was sixteen weeks in January. As of Monday, Kaneda has put us on allocation and the quote is twenty-two weeks with no commitment on quantity above four units per month.

Greg Salinas: Four units a month is two machines a month. The FY26 plan says we build seven EFEMs a month in the second half.

Nadia Haddad: Correct. That is the problem.

Yuki Tanaka: Why are we on allocation? Did we do something?

Nadia Haddad: No. Kaneda's own capacity is going to a handful of very large customers in the wafer fab equipment space and we are a small account for them. We buy maybe 300 units a year. Their large customers buy that in a month.

Priya Raman: Options.

Nadia Haddad: Three. One, accept allocation and cap our build rate at two machines a month, which means we miss the FY26 revenue plan. Two, pay for expedite, which Kaneda has quoted at a 40 percent premium and which still caps at six units a month. Three, qualify a second source.

Tomas Lindqvist: A second source on a harmonic drive is not a drop-in. The reducer sets the arm's backlash and its torsional stiffness, which is what our repeatability number rests on. Changing it means rerunning repeatability qualification and rerunning the particle qualification, because a different grease and a different seal design changes the outgassing and the particle behaviour.

Nadia Haddad: Halstead Drive in the UK will quote us. Their unit is dimensionally compatible and their catalogue backlash is one arc-minute against Kaneda's 0.7.

Tomas Lindqvist: One arc-minute at the R axis is roughly 30 micrometres at the wafer at full extension. Our spec is plus or minus 25. So the answer on paper is no.

Nadia Haddad: On paper. Halstead has a selected-pair grade they do not publish, which they claim holds 0.6.

Tomas Lindqvist: Then we test it. I am not qualifying a reducer on a claim.

Yuki Tanaka: What does a qualification cost and how long?

Tomas Lindqvist: Five months and roughly 140,000 dollars, most of which is my engineer's time and cleanroom hours for the particle re-qualification. That is a real number, not a guess, because we did exactly this on the previous generation.

Yuki Tanaka: Five months means it does not help Helios-3 first customer ship at all.

Tomas Lindqvist: Correct. It helps us in 2027.

Priya Raman: Then we are choosing between expedite and a slower build rate for this year, and separately deciding whether to start a qualification that pays off next year.

Yuki Tanaka: Expedite at 40 percent on a reducer that costs what?

Nadia Haddad: 4,200 dollars each, two per machine. So the premium is about 3,400 dollars per machine, against a machine that sells for 620,000.

Yuki Tanaka: Then it is not a money question, it is a capacity question. Pay the expedite.

Priya Raman: Decision one: we pay Kaneda's expedite premium for FY26 Helios-3 builds. Decision two: we fund the Halstead second-source qualification starting in Q3, 140,000 dollars, targeting qualification complete in Q1 2027. Nadia, place the expedite order this week.

Nadia Haddad: Placing it this week. I will also ask Kaneda for a rolling twelve-month forecast agreement, which sometimes moves you up the allocation list.

Nadia Haddad: Second item, Brunnen linear encoder heads. Eleven weeks, single source, no allocation problem, but Brunnen has told us they are end-of-lifing the specific head we designed in. Last-time-buy notice is expected in the autumn.

Tomas Lindqvist: That is a design change either way then. Better to do it deliberately in Rev B than in a panic.

Nadia Haddad: Third item, the Cerapoint ceramic blades. Thirteen weeks, single source, and I have negotiated the buffer stock Priya asked for. Eight weeks of blades held on our floor, which is 46 blades, about 71,000 dollars of inventory.

Yuki Tanaka: 71,000 dollars sitting on a shelf. What is the alternative cost?

Nadia Haddad: A blade shortage stops a build. A stopped build on a 620,000 dollar machine near quarter end is worse than 71,000 dollars of inventory.

Yuki Tanaka: Approved, and I want it in the FY26 working capital line explicitly, not buried in inventory. Owen will pick it up in the budget review.

Greg Salinas: One more thing nobody has said. All three of these parts are in the EFEM. None of them are in Kestrel-2. If we get squeezed, Kestrel builds keep going and Helios builds stop, and our revenue is weighted the other way.

Priya Raman: Say that again at the ops review in August with the build plan next to it.
