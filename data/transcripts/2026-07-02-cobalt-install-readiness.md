# Cobalt Foundry Install Readiness

Victor Nwosu: Cobalt's EFEM install is scheduled for the eleventh of August at their Dresden site. This is the readiness check, six weeks out.

Greg Salinas: The unit is built. It is machine 004, which is one of the four that needs the top plate gasket rework kit from the Northgate 8D.

Ingrid Lund: Then it does not ship until the rework is done and I have measured the box. I am not sending a machine to a second-source evaluation with the defect we just apologised for at another account.

Greg Salinas: The kit is in hand. Rework is four hours. Ingrid needs the box for the recovery measurement after.

Ingrid Lund: Ninety-minute test. Schedule it and I will do it.

Greg Salinas: I will schedule the rework and the particle measurement for the week of the thirteenth of July.

Claire Dubois: Dresden means CE marking, not just S2. The CE file reuses most of the S2 work, but the declaration of conformity has to be signed against the machinery directive and the EMC directive, and EMC testing is not done.

Victor Nwosu: Is EMC a problem?

Claire Dubois: Not usually, but it is a lab booking and a real test. Three days at an accredited lab, and the earliest slot I can get is the twenty-first of July.

Ryan Chen: What happens if the machine lands in Dresden without a CE declaration?

Claire Dubois: Then it is not legal to put into service in the European Union and Cobalt's own EHS team will refuse it at the door. They will not be flexible about it, and they should not be.

Ryan Chen: Then the twenty-first of July slot is not optional.

Claire Dubois: I have a provisional hold on it. I will confirm the EMC lab booking tomorrow and it costs 9,000 euros.

Aisha Bello: Install logistics. Who is on site and for how long?

Victor Nwosu: Me for the install week, plus one of Greg's technicians. Cobalt provides the facilities hookup, we provide the tool and the teach.

Aisha Bello: And after install? Because the APAC field service hire starts in September and Dresden is not APAC anyway. If something breaks in Dresden in October, who goes?

Victor Nwosu: Me, from wherever I am.

Aisha Bello: That is not a service model, that is a person. I am raising it again because it will be a real gap during a second-source evaluation where every incident is scored.

Ryan Chen: Cobalt is scoring us. They have said explicitly that response time is part of the evaluation.

Aisha Bello: Then I want an agreed response commitment before install, in writing, that we can actually meet. If it is 48 hours on site in Europe, say 48 hours. Do not say 24 because it sounds better.

Victor Nwosu: Forty-eight is honest from a standing start. Twenty-four is only true if someone is already in Europe.

Aisha Bello: Then 48 goes in the install agreement, and I will look at a European service partner for FY27.

Ryan Chen: I can sell 48 hours. I cannot sell an apology in October.

Victor Nwosu: Last item. Cobalt asked whether the Helios-3 particle specification is per pass or per wafer end to end, because their acceptance test will measure end to end across nine passes.

Ingrid Lund: Our specification is three adders per pass. Nine passes at three adders is 27, which would fail almost any fab's incoming criteria if you read it that way, and it is also not how it behaves, because adders do not accumulate linearly.

Victor Nwosu: What do they actually measure?

Ingrid Lund: In my experience a nine-pass cycle on a healthy machine lands around six to eight total, not 27. But I have never measured nine passes on Helios-3 specifically. I have measured one, fifty times.

Ryan Chen: Then somebody should measure nine before their acceptance test, not during it.

Ingrid Lund: Agreed, and I will do it on machine 004 before it ships. If the answer is bad I would rather find out in Dresden's absence.

Aisha Bello: Please put the number in writing when you have it, so that whatever we tell Cobalt at acceptance matches what we told them at install.
